# The plan we had in mind

When we were expecting our second daughter, we thought we knew how those first few weeks would look.

We’d done the preparation. We’d read the books, attended the hypnobirthing classes and hoped for another vaginal birth. We planned to exclusively breastfeed, just as we’d intended with our first. It felt like part of the picture we’d built in our heads throughout pregnancy.

Then reality arrived.

The first few feeds went well enough. We thought we’d settled into it.

But over the next couple of days, every feed became more painful. Not the discomfort everyone tells you to expect, but pain that made us dread the next time our daughter needed feeding. Those early newborn moments that we’d imagined would be calm and precious quickly became something we feared.

My wife was exhausted and in pain.

I felt largely useless.

Outside of changing nappies, winding our daughter and trying to look after our eldest, there wasn’t much I could actually do. Watching someone you love struggle while knowing you can’t take the pain away is a difficult place to be.

We discovered our daughter had a tongue tie.

We’d been through the process of having it divided with our first daughter. For us, it wasn’t an experience we wanted to repeat. It was emotionally difficult, and this time we felt we had enough experience to make a different decision. Every family will make the choice that’s right for them, but ours was to leave it alone and adapt around it.

So that’s exactly what we did.

Instead of holding onto the picture we’d created during pregnancy, we built one that worked for the family standing in front of us.

Breastfeeding didn’t stop.

It simply stopped being the only way we fed.

We introduced formula when we needed it. We started expressing milk. I could give bottles while my wife rested. Nipple shields made breastfeeding dramatically more comfortable, and instead of every feed being something to endure, they gradually became something we could enjoy again.

The guilt didn’t disappear overnight.

It’s hard to let go of the idea that feeding has to happen one specific way. But the more we focused on what our daughter actually needed, rather than what we’d imagined before she arrived, the easier that guilt became to carry.

Today, she’s gaining weight well. She’s alert, content and thriving.

My wife is happier.

I’m able to take an active role in feeding our daughter instead of standing on the sidelines feeling helpless.

And those precious first weeks are no longer overshadowed by anxiety about the next feed.

One unexpected consequence of all this was our app.

We originally built it around breastfeeding because that’s what we expected our own journey to be. Within days of our daughter being born, we realised our own needs had changed.

Suddenly we weren’t just tracking breastfeeding. We were tracking bottles, expressed milk and pumping too.

So we changed the app to reflect the reality we were living.

Because that’s what parenting has taught us more than anything:

Very few families follow the plan they made during pregnancy.

Plans change.

Babies surprise you.

Sometimes your feeding journey looks nothing like you expected.

That doesn’t mean you’ve failed.

It simply means you’ve adapted.

If you’re reading this while feeling guilty because your feeding journey hasn’t gone to plan, know this: your baby won’t remember whether every feed came directly from the breast, a bottle of expressed milk or formula.

They’ll remember growing up loved, fed and cared for.

And that’s always been the goal.
