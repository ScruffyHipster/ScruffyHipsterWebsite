A social media blocker for iPhone should block both the app and the website path. Otherwise the habit can move from the icon to the browser. The goal is not to make your phone useless. It is to stop one quick check from becoming a long scroll.

Start with the services that cost you the most time. Do not block every social app just because you can. Block the ones that interrupt work, studying, sleep, or time with people.

# How to block social media apps on iPhone

The built-in way is a Screen Time App Limit on the Social category:

1. Open **Settings › Screen Time › App Limits** and tap **Add Limit**.
2. Select **Social**, or open it and pick only the apps that cost you time.
3. Set a short daily allowance, such as one minute for a near-complete block, and tap **Add**.

To block social media only at certain times, use **Downtime** instead and leave messaging apps under **Always Allowed**. Set a Screen Time passcode if you want **Ignore Limit** to stop being a one-tap way out.

# How to block social media websites in Safari

Blocking the app is not enough if the same feed is one search away in Safari. In **Settings › Screen Time › Content & Privacy Restrictions**, open **App Store, Media, Web & Games › Web Content**, choose **Limit Adult Websites**, then add sites such as instagram.com, tiktok.com or x.com under **Never Allow**. # Useful social media blocking rules

- Block the app and the matching website.
- Use timed sessions for work, study, or evening breaks.
- Keep allowed apps available if you still need messages, maps, banking, or calls.
- Review when blocked attempts happen.
- Adjust the block list instead of making the first setup extreme.

Small reliable rules usually beat dramatic rules you disable immediately.

Blocking does not mean you can never use social media. It means you decide when it belongs in the day. Put a focus window around the times where it tends to take over, then leave intentional time outside that window if you want it.

Yes. Screen Time is built into iPhone and can block social apps and websites at no cost. Apps such as Rewire are free to download with in-app purchases, so check which controls the free version includes.

Yes. Block the feed apps, then keep messaging apps available: under Always Allowed in Downtime, or on the allow list of a focus session in Rewire.
