To block apps on iPhone, you can use Apple's built-in Screen Time controls or an app blocker that works with Screen Time. The basic pattern is the same: choose the apps that distract you, decide when blocking should run, and make access harder during that window.

Yes, you can block apps on iPhone without installing anything. Screen Time gives you three useful tools: App Limits for a daily allowance, Downtime for a schedule, and a passcode so the block is harder to dismiss. iOS 18 and later can also lock an individual app behind Face ID.

# Block an app with Screen Time App Limits

App Limits block an app once you have used it for a set time each day. Set the limit to one minute and it works as a near-complete block.

1. Open **Settings** and tap **Screen Time**.
2. Tap **App Limits**, then **Add Limit**.
3. Choose a category such as Social, or open a category and select individual apps.
4. Tap **Next**, set the daily time allowed, and tap **Add**.

When the limit runs out, the app’s icon dims and opening it shows a Screen Time screen instead.

# Block apps at certain times with Downtime

Downtime blocks everything except the apps you allow, on a schedule you set. It suits evenings, study blocks or the first hour of the day.

1. In **Settings › Screen Time**, tap **Downtime**.
2. Turn on **Scheduled** and choose the days and times.
3. Go back and open **Always Allowed** to keep calls, messages, maps or anything else you need.

# Stop yourself overriding the block

By default, Screen Time lets you tap **Ignore Limit** and carry on. To make the block firmer, set a Screen Time passcode with **Lock Screen Time Settings**, ideally one you don’t know by heart. With a passcode on, App Limits and Downtime offer **Block at End of Limit** and **Block at Downtime**, which ask for the code before granting more time.

# Lock or hide an app with Face ID

On iOS 18 and later, touch and hold an app’s icon and choose **Require Face ID** (or Touch ID or passcode). You can lock the app or hide it in a hidden folder. This keeps an app private rather than limiting how much you use it, so it is less useful for breaking a habit.

# Block built-in apps such as Safari or Camera

Some Apple apps can be switched off entirely. In **Settings › Screen Time › Content & Privacy Restrictions**, turn the restrictions on, then use **Allowed Apps** to turn off apps like Safari or Camera. They disappear from the Home Screen until you turn them back on.

# When a dedicated blocker helps

A dedicated app blocker helps when you want faster focus sessions, app and website lists that match different parts of your day, or an interruption that makes the reflex visible before the app opens.

That matters because many distracting opens are not planned. You pick up the phone for one thing, tap a familiar icon, and only notice the detour later.

1. Pick three to five apps that most often interrupt you.
2. Add websites if the same distraction moves into Safari.

Start small. A blocker works best when it matches the real habit rather than a perfect version of your day.

# Common questions

## Can you block apps on iPhone without Screen Time?

Not completely. Third-party app blockers, including Rewire, use Apple’s Screen Time permission to shield apps, so Screen Time access is part of their setup. Without it, the closest option is locking an app with Face ID.

## Does blocking an app delete it or its data?

No. A blocked app stays installed with its data intact. It simply shows a block screen until the limit, schedule or session ends.

## How do I block an app permanently?

Delete it, or set a one-minute App Limit protected by a Screen Time passcode someone else keeps. For built-in apps, turn them off under Allowed Apps.
