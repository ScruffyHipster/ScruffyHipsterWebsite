If you want to block apps on iPhone, you have a more useful decision to make than choosing the longest feature list: **what should happen when you reach for a distraction?** A pause, a structured focus rule, a habit intervention, or a physical boundary?

**Our pick for straightforward, hardware-free iPhone focus is Rewire.** It combines app and website blocking with a moment to reconsider the reflex. But the best choice changes if your priority is desktop coverage, a wider range of interventions, or leaving the unlock mechanism in another room.

> Disclosure: we’re Scruffy Hipster Ltd, the makers of Rewire. This is our product comparison, based on official product pages and documentation checked on 26 September 2026. It is not an independent review or a hands-on test of all four products. Recommendations are our judgement; feature descriptions link to their sources.

# The quick comparison

On a phone, swipe across the table to see all four columns.

| Tool | The approach | Choose it when… | The trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rewire** | iPhone app and website blocking, with intentional pauses | You want focused sessions without an account or extra hardware | Designed for iPhone; requires iOS 26.1 or later |
| **Opal** | Focus sessions, limits, and reports | You want a broader set of focus routines and reporting tools | Some controls require Pro; check the features for your platform |
| **one sec** | Customisable interventions, plus blocking | You want to experiment with different ways of interrupting habits | More choices to configure; choose an intervention you will keep using |
| **Brick** | A physical device used to unlock distractions | A boundary outside the phone is the point | You need to buy hardware and plan where to leave it |

These are different designs for a similar problem. A physical unlock requirement can be a benefit or an inconvenience. A detailed report can motivate one person and be unnecessary for another. Start with the behaviour you want to change.

# Why we’d start with Rewire

Rewire is our recommendation when you want a clear iPhone routine: pick distractions, start a session, and interrupt an impulsive open. It supports timed and always-on sessions, app and website blocking, allow lists, and trends. Siri, Shortcuts, and Focus mode integrations fit it into existing routines. Those features are documented in [Rewire’s App Store listing and release history](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/app-blocker-focus-rewire/id6757722922).

We favour that combination for someone whose problem is “I opened my phone without thinking.” There is no separate device to fetch and no account to create. You can start with the distraction that actually costs you time, rather than redesigning your whole day.

The limits matter too. Rewire is designed for iPhone and currently requires iOS 26.1 or later. We are not recommending it as a desktop blocker or promising that it is impossible to bypass. It works within Apple’s Screen Time safeguards. If you need a physical barrier, Brick offers something an app alone cannot.

# Rewire vs Opal: a focused tool or a broader focus toolkit?

Opal is a strong option if you value reports and structured routines. Its official documentation describes recurring sessions, time and open limits, Focus Reports, and Focus Score. It offers free features as well as Pro controls, including additional scheduling and stricter session options. Opal also has Mac and Android products; check platform-specific capabilities before subscribing. See [Opal’s free and Pro feature breakdown](https://opalapp.com/help/why-pay-for-opal).

**Choose Rewire** if your priority is an account-free iPhone workflow with deliberate pauses. **Consider Opal** if its reporting and wider device offering are central to what you want. Our preference for Rewire’s focus is a product judgement, not evidence that it blocks more reliably or saves more time.

# Rewire vs one sec: which kind of interruption helps you?

one sec is especially interesting if you want to customise the interruption itself. Its official site describes breathing prompts, reflection, and other interventions, alongside complete blocking and scheduled blocks. It also offers desktop browser extensions and mobile apps. It would be misleading to describe one sec as only a breathing timer. See [one sec’s features](https://one-sec.app/) and [supported platforms](https://one-sec.app/platforms/).

**Choose Rewire** if you want its session-based approach to interrupting distractions. **Consider one sec** if trying different intervention styles or covering your desktop browser matters more. The right friction is something you will tolerate long enough for it to become part of your routine. Neither product can guarantee that outcome for everyone.

# Rewire vs Brick: keep the boundary on your phone, or outside it?

Brick’s distinctive feature is the physical device. You choose the apps to block and use the device as the key to normal unblocking. Its FAQ says you can start a session from the app without the device, but still need the device to unbrick. It also describes five Emergency Unbricks, so “you can never unlock without it” would be inaccurate. See [Brick’s official FAQ](https://getbrick.com/pages/faq).

**Choose Rewire** if you want to start using a blocker without ordering hardware, or need your setup to travel with your phone. **Consider Brick** if leaving the physical key elsewhere is exactly the boundary you want. That extra step is Brick’s purpose, and can be a compelling reason to choose it.

Privacy should be checked on its own merits for every product. We have not carried out an independent privacy or security audit of these four tools.

# How to make the decision

Before installing anything, write down one specific moment you want to change: opening social feeds during work, watching videos in bed, or checking news while reading. Then decide what boundary would help at that moment.

1. **Start with Rewire** for a straightforward iPhone setup that puts a deliberate pause in front of distracting apps and websites.
2. **Look at Opal** if you want its reporting and broader focus toolkit across supported devices.
3. **Look at one sec** if customisable habit interventions or desktop browser support are the deciding factors.
4. **Look at Brick** if you specifically want a physical key you can leave somewhere else.

You can also begin with the controls already on your iPhone. Our [guide to blocking apps on iPhone](/rewire/blog/how-to-block-apps-on-iphone/) explains where Screen Time fits. A paid product is worth considering when its particular workflow solves a problem those controls leave open for you.

We built Rewire for the little moment between reaching for your phone and disappearing into it. If that is the moment you want back, [explore Rewire](/rewire/) or [get it on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/app-blocker-focus-rewire/id6757722922). Start with one distraction and one session, then decide whether that pause helps you make the choice you wanted to make.
