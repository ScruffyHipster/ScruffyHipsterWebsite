# Rewire: our pick for account-free focus sessions

[Rewire](/rewire/) combines app and website blocking with timed or always-on sessions. Allow lists can keep selected essentials available, while Siri, Shortcuts and Focus mode integrations let sessions fit existing routines. Its [App Store listing and release history](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/app-blocker-focus-rewire/id6757722922) document these features.

We would start here if your goal is to protect a work session, a chapter of a book, or time with other people without creating an account or buying hardware. Rewire is designed for iPhone, so it is not our recommendation for someone primarily seeking desktop blocking.

Rewire has no ads or accounts. Its [privacy policy](/privacy/rewire/) explains the limited anonymous analytics used to improve the app; account-free does not mean analytics-free. It is free to download with in-app purchases, rather than a promise that every feature is free.

# Opal: consider it for routines and reporting

Opal documents focus sessions, time limits, open limits, Focus Reports and Focus Score. Its [free-versus-Pro guide](https://opalapp.com/help/why-pay-for-opal) lists three recurring sessions in the free version, with unlimited recurring sessions and additional controls in Pro. Opal also offers Mac and Android products, with platform-specific details to check.

Our judgement: consider Opal if recurring routines and reviewing your focus data are central to how you want to use a blocker. Compare the features you need against the current plan before paying. A longer feature list does not by itself establish better blocking or more time saved.

# one sec: consider it for customisable interventions

[one sec’s official site](https://one-sec.app/) describes different interventions, including breathing and reflection, alongside complete blocks and scheduling. It also offers desktop browser extensions. Describing it as only a breathing timer would miss part of its offering.

Our judgement: consider one sec when the automatic act of opening an app is the habit you want to notice, and you want to explore different interruptions. Check the configuration carefully: allowing a pause before continuing and preventing access during a block are different outcomes. Neither is automatically the right choice for every situation.

# Brick: consider it for physical separation

Brick uses a physical device for normal unblocking. Its [official FAQ](https://getbrick.com/pages/faq) says you can start blocking without the device, but need the device to unbrick. It also describes Emergency Unbricks, so this is not an absolute guarantee that access can never be restored without the hardware.

Our judgement: consider Brick if leaving the key in another room is the boundary you actually want. The hardware is the reason to choose it. If you frequently need to adjust your focus while travelling, think through where the device will be and what you need to keep available. Check purchase and delivery costs for your location.

# Apple Screen Time: start here for built-in limits

Apple includes App Limits, Downtime and Always Allowed settings on iPhone. Its [Screen Time guide](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/guide/iphone/iphb0c7313c9/ios) explains schedules, time allowances and blocking options.

Our judgement: try these first if your requirement is simply a daily allowance or a regular period away from selected apps. There is no separate blocker to buy. Review the passcode and blocking settings instead of assuming that creating a time limit makes it impossible to extend. A dedicated app becomes useful when its particular session or interruption workflow solves something your current setup leaves unresolved.

# How to choose without installing everything

Write down one situation you want to change, then use that situation to compare options. “I want fewer distractions” is hard to evaluate. “I want to read for 30 minutes without opening social feeds” gives you a concrete task.

Before committing to a paid plan or hardware, check:

1. **Coverage:** can you block the app and the website where you repeat the same habit?
2. **Timing:** do you need an immediate session, a recurring schedule, or an always-on boundary?
3. **Essentials:** can the calls, tools and websites you need remain available?
4. **Ending a session:** what happens when you want to stop, take a break, or change the rules?
5. **Cost:** does the exact feature you need require payment, renewal, or hardware?

This is a checklist for your own trial, not a claim that we completed those tests across the shortlist. Compare one ordinary situation before changing your whole routine. Our [guide to blocking apps on iPhone](/rewire/blog/how-to-block-apps-on-iphone/) is a starting point for setup.

# Common questions

## What is the best free app blocker for iPhone?

Apple Screen Time is the built-in starting point without an additional purchase. A free app download can still include paid features. Check the actual limits and controls available before treating Rewire, Opal or one sec as a completely free replacement.

## Which should I choose for studying?

Start with the study task and what needs to remain accessible. We would consider Rewire for an iPhone focus session, then compare alternatives if recurring reports, different interventions, or a physical key matter more. See our [study focus guide](/rewire/blog/focus-app-for-studying/) for a practical starting point.

## Can these apps guarantee I will stop scrolling?

No outcome like that is established by this comparison. Blocking controls, your settings and the way you use the tool all matter. We have not ranked bypass resistance, carried out a security audit, or measured time saved across these products.
